Exploring alternative halo finders for approximate cosmological simulations

Marina Cagliari

View details

Tests whether a faster way to identify dark matter halos can recover cosmological information from approximate simulations.

The large volumes of modern spectroscopic surveys make building the large suites of simulations required for simulation-based inference (SBI) extremely challenging. In this project I explore alternatives to accurate but costly gravitational solvers and halo finders for preparing halo catalogs for SBI analyses.

In particular, I apply the relaxed Friends-of-Friends (rFoF) halo finder to fast particle-mesh FastPM simulations. rFoF is a modified version of the standard Friends-of-Friends (FoF) finder, made more flexible by a set of free parameters that can be tuned to reproduce the halo clustering of high-resolution simulations. I train SBI models on FoF and rFoF halo catalogs, jointly inferring cosmology and the rFoF parameters, and test them on Quijote N-body simulations with Rockstar halo catalogs. The aim is to assess whether rFoF improves on FoF and whether this approach could be applied to real-data analyses.

QuassiQ: Identify changing-look Quasars

Prisha Desai, Kanyuni Iemoto

View details

Looks for changing-look quasar candidates in DESI DR1 spectra by tracking changes in how a machine learning model represents each spectrum.

We apply SpenderQ, a machine learning framework, to DESI DR1 quasar spectra and explore variability in its latent representations to identify additional changing-look quasar candidates. This approach complements existing detection methods based on flux variability.

Metal Absorber: Map metal absorber systems

Prisha Desai, Ananya Srivastava

View details

The circumgalactic and intergalactic medium (CGM/IGM) encodes key signatures of galaxy formation, including gas accretion and feedback-driven outflows, and is thought to contain the majority of baryons in the Universe.

Absorption spectroscopy is a powerful probe of this diffuse gas, but detecting absorption lines depends on accurately reconstructing the intrinsic continuum of the background source. We use SpenderQ, a spectral autoencoder, to estimate quasar continua and build an automated pipeline that detects and identifies metal absorption lines in quasar spectra. We apply this framework to the unprecedented volume of quasar spectra from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) to compile a catalog of metal absorber systems, including Mg II and Fe II.
